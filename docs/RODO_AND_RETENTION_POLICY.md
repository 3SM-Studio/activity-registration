## 2. Scope and minimization

The public form collects only the data required to receive a registration request, determine the minor/adult flow, support age-appropriate group matching and contact the applicant.

Collected public data:

- city and requested Offering,
- participant first and last name,
- participant date of birth,
- guardian first and last name for a minor,
- phone,
- e-mail.

Operational metadata includes technical identifiers, timestamps, season, snapshots, workflow status, optional assigned group, duplicate reference and narrowly scoped operator notes.

Do not collect through this process:

- PESEL,
- home address,
- school,
- health information,
- diagnoses or medication,
- disability details,
- religion,
- image/marketing consent,
- billing data,
- criminal-record information about staff.

## 3. Purposes and legal bases

### 3.1 Registration request and pre-contractual contact

Purpose:

- receive the request,
- verify whether an appropriate group may be offered,
- contact the adult participant or guardian,
- propose a concrete group/time and participation arrangement.

Basis: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR, processing necessary to take steps at the data subject's request before entering into an agreement.

### 3.2 Group matching and minor-flow operation

Purpose:

- use date of birth to determine the guardian flow,
- support human age-group matching,
- preserve accurate season/offering context.

Basis: Article 6(1)(b) where directly necessary to handle the requested participation arrangement and, where supplementary operational processing is needed, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.

### 3.3 Duplicate prevention, service security and operational integrity

Purpose:

- avoid obvious duplicate submissions,
- detect likely duplicate records for human review,
- maintain technical idempotency,
- diagnose failures without logging form content,
- protect the public endpoint from abuse.

Basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, legitimate interest in maintaining a reliable and secure registration system.

### 3.4 Claims and accountability

Purpose:

- establish, pursue or defend claims,
- demonstrate what registration request and participation arrangement were handled,
- respond to privacy/security incidents where necessary.

Basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR and Article 6(1)(c) where a specific legal obligation applies.

No generic `I agree to GDPR` consent is used for the core intake purpose.

## 4. Legitimate Interest Assessment

This section documents the Article 6(1)(f) balancing assessment for the v3 process.

### 4.1 Interest test

The interests are specific, lawful, real and current:

- secure and reliable operation of a registration system requested by applicants,
- avoiding accidental duplicate records and operational confusion,
- defending Pozytywka and applicants against inaccurate records or disputes,
- maintaining minimal evidence of system actions.

### 4.2 Necessity test

The processing is limited to data already needed for the registration process or technical identifiers generated by the system.

Less intrusive safeguards are used:

- no public person-search endpoint,
- no reversible PII fingerprint stored merely for deduplication,
- no health/special-category data,
- no request bodies in logs,
- duplicate comparison runs server-side,
- technical IDs are preferred in diagnostics,
- no automatic group assignment or automated acceptance.

### 4.3 Balancing test

Applicants reasonably expect Pozytywka to process the details they intentionally submit in order to respond to the request. Additional safeguards reduce the impact on minors:

- date of birth has a documented age-matching/guardian purpose,
- a human makes the final group decision,
- access is restricted,
- retention is finite,
- no marketing use is attached to the registration form,
- no special-category data is requested,
- a guardian contact is required for minors,
- probable duplicates are not exposed publicly.

The interests and rights of the participant, particularly a child, are reviewed as part of this assessment. On the current narrow scope and safeguards, the legitimate interests do not override those rights. Re-run this assessment if new data categories, profiling, analytics, marketing or automated decision-making are introduced.

## 5. Retention schedule

Retention is status/outcome based.

| Outcome                         | Retention in registration intake store                                                                | Reason                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `NEW`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONTACTED` | while actively handled; if closed without confirmation, up to 12 months from closure                  | handling, correction and short post-process accountability          |
| `WAITLISTED`                    | through the relevant season plus 3 months                                                             | possible place becoming available and orderly closure of the season |
| `REJECTED`                      | 12 months from rejection                                                                              | short-term correction/dispute handling and repeat-contact context   |
| `CANCELLED`                     | 12 months from cancellation                                                                           | short-term correction/dispute handling                              |
| `CONFIRMED`                     | until the end of the calendar year in which 3 years have elapsed after the end of the relevant season | ordinary business-claim limitation and accountability               |

If a concrete dispute, legal hold or statutory obligation requires longer storage, preserve only the data necessary for that purpose and record the reason.

At the end of retention, delete or irreversibly anonymize the registration record. Do not extend retention merely because deletion is inconvenient.

Confirmed participants may be moved into a separate participation/accounting process with its own purposes and retention. That separate process must not silently inherit the registration Sheet forever.

## 9. Data subject rights procedure

Requests may be sent to `pozytywka.boleslaw@gmail.com`.

For each request:

1. record receipt date and request type with minimal data,
2. verify identity proportionately,
3. locate relevant Production records,
4. determine the applicable right and any lawful exception,
5. perform access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability or objection action as applicable,
6. respond through the verified contact channel,
7. record minimal completion evidence.

Do not paste participant records into GitHub issues, general chat channels or public screenshots.

Rights include, depending on the processing basis and circumstances:

- access,
- rectification,
- erasure,
- restriction,
- portability for eligible processing,
- objection to Article 6(1)(f) processing,
- complaint to the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes UODO).

## 10. Security and incidents

- no participant PII in application logs,
- no service-account private keys committed to Git,
- Vercel OIDC/WIF for Google access,
- distinct TEST and PROD Google identities,
- least-privilege Sheet sharing,
- security headers, server validation, honeypot, minimum-fill-time and rate limiting,
- incident logs should use technical request/registration IDs rather than names/contact data.

If a suspected personal-data breach occurs:

1. contain access or erroneous processing,
2. preserve technical evidence without unnecessarily copying participant data,
3. identify categories/scale and likely consequences,
4. notify Iwona immediately,
5. assess supervisory-authority and data-subject notification duties under GDPR,
6. document the decision and remediation.
